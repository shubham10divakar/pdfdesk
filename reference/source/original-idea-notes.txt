What you're describing is actually a very interesting idea! I would call it a PDF Research Workspace or Research Paper IDE.

You don't simply want a PDF reader. You want a centralized environment where you can manage your entire reading and research lifecycle.

Think of it as combining:

- VS Code: A workspace for organizing files and projects.
- Zotero: A reference manager for research papers.
- Notion: A system for tracking tasks, status and notes.
- PDF reader: For reading, highlighting, annotating and bookmarking.
- Reading tracker: To remember what you read, when you read it, and what remains.

The important distinction is that your PDFs are not just files. Each PDF represents a piece of knowledge that moves through a lifecycle.

I'd want a three-panel interface:

| Panel  | Purpose                                                     |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Left   | PDF library, folders, tags, reading queue                   |
| Center | PDF viewer with highlighting and annotations                |
| Right  | Personal notes, extracted highlights, research observations |

The right panel should be independent of the PDF page length. Your notes might become 10–20 pages while the paper itself remains open.

You should also be able to:

- Jump from a note to its original PDF page.
- Highlight text using different colors.
- Add comments to specific paragraphs.
- Extract all highlights into a structured note.
- Bookmark important pages.
- Maintain separate notes for each paper.

## 3. Reading management — the most important feature

I would introduce a proper reading lifecycle.

| Status    | Meaning                 |
| --------- | ----------------------- |
| Inbox     | Imported, not evaluated |
| Planned   | Intend to read          |
| Reading   | Currently reading       |
| Paused    | Started but postponed   |
| Completed | Finished reading        |
| Revisit   | Need to read again      |

Each paper should maintain its own reading history.

For example:

Attention Is All You Need

Reading

Progress: 80%

Reading sessions

1. November 3, 2026

   Pages 9–12 · 35 minutes

   Understood scaled dot-product attention.
2. November 2, 2026

   Pages 5–8 · 40 minutes

   Studied multi-head attention.
3. October 31, 2026

   Pages 1–4 · 25 minutes

   Introduction and motivation.

Next reading target

Page 13 · November 4

Planned

This gives you something that ordinary PDF readers generally don't emphasize: a historical record of your intellectual progress.

## 5. Features I'd add that make it genuinely different

Beyond the existing products, I'd add:

1. Reading calendar: See what you read on each day, week or month.
2. Reading backlog: Papers planned for this week, next week and later.
3. Automatic resume: Reopen a paper exactly where you stopped.
4. Reading analytics: Pages read, hours spent, papers completed and unfinished papers.
5. Research connections: Connect papers by topic, methodology, architecture and findings.
6. Revision queue: Resurface important highlights from previously read papers.
7. AI-assisted notes: Generate a structured summary from your own annotations, with page references.

## 6. One possible application architecture

If you decide to build this yourself:

| Component           | Technology              |
| ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Desktop application | Tauri + React           |
| PDF rendering       | PDF.js                  |
| Local database      | SQLite                  |
| PDF annotations     | PDF.js annotation layer |
| Search              | SQLite FTS5             |
| Notes               | Markdown editor         |
| Analytics           | SQL queries             |
| AI features         | Local or API-based LLM  |

I'd make it local-first, so your PDFs, notes and reading history remain under your control.
